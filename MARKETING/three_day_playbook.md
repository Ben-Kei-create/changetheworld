# TERRA — 3-Day Playbook
## How to let TERRA speak for itself

Prepared by: Hiro Matsumoto, Marketing
Input from: The whole team, and ???

---

### The only rule

**Do not sell. Do not explain. Do not ask them to buy anything.**

The goal for 3 days is to create conditions where they want to experience TERRA — and then let TERRA do the rest. If at any point it feels like a pitch, you've lost. If they ask what it is, say: *"Something we've been working on. Tell us what you think."*

---

## Day 1 — The question nobody explained

**Morning (before anything else):**

Ask each person from the other team — individually, casually, before any demo or explanation:

> *"Before we show you anything — where did you come from?"*

Don't give context. If they ask what you mean, say: *"Anywhere you want. A place. A person. A moment."* Write their answers on paper. Keep them.

**Why this works:** They'll answer because it's disarming. The question has no obvious agenda. It's personal and specific. They'll wonder about it later.

---

**The rest of Day 1:**

Send them this link. Say nothing except: *"Try this when you have 20 minutes alone."*

```
[your domain]/demo.html
```

Do not follow up. Do not ask what they thought. Let it sit overnight.

**What the demo does:**
- Asks them the origin question again (so they answer twice — once unannounced, once in context)
- Puts them inside Hana's story in 3 scenes
- Shows them real research data without lecturing
- Asks what they feel afterward
- Reflects their written answer back to them at the end

The experience takes 15–25 minutes. It is designed so that when it ends, the last thing on screen is: *This is $19.99. One time.*

---

## Day 2 — Let them come to you

**Do not initiate.** If they bring it up (and they will), listen more than you speak. Some things to have ready:

**If they say "I liked the Tanaka-san scene":**
> "That came from the Cacioppo & Patrick loneliness research. Every detail in the story has a citation. The credits screen lists every paper."

**If they say "Is the whole game like this?":**
> "Seven stories. Six more characters. Each one ends the same way — your origin answer comes back."

**If they ask about the app:**
> Show them the website. Just the homepage. Let them click around.
> "It's native macOS. No internet connection needed. No data leaves the device. The privacy policy is four sentences."

**If they say "How much?":**
> Don't answer yet. Say: "We'll get to that tomorrow."

**If someone is skeptical / seems unimpressed:**
That person might be the most important one. Ask them what they'd want to see that would change their mind. Then actually listen. Don't argue.

**In the evening (optional):**
If they're open to it, show them the real-world data behind one more character — Astrid's story (climate anxiety). Share the Lancet 2021 statistic. *"59% of young people globally are very or extremely worried about climate change. 45% say it affects their daily life. We built a story around that feeling — not to explain it, but to be inside it."*

---

## Day 3 — The mirror

**Morning:**

Take out the papers from Day 1. Read their answers back to them — in a room, together.

Say: *"Three days ago, before you knew anything about TERRA, we asked where you came from. Here's what you said."*

Then show them the end of the demo — the mirror screen — so they see how the mechanic works in the full game. *"In TERRA, this happens after all 7 stories. You wrote something at the very beginning. And then it comes back."*

---

**The pitch that isn't a pitch:**

Show them the app (if it's compiled) or the website. Walk them through what exists:

- 7 complete stories, 3 scenes each, all from real research
- The credits screen (show it) — names, sources, ???, GodManager, the baby
- The privacy policy: *"We don't collect your data. Full stop."*
- Settings: text size, motion reduction, high contrast — accessibility built in
- $19.99. One time. macOS native.

Then stop. Don't close. Don't ask for a decision. Leave the room.

---

**What happens:**

Some of them will buy it without being asked. Some will ask "where can I get it?" before you've said the price. Some will want to show it to someone else.

The ones who don't buy it — watch what they do. Do they keep asking questions? Do they want to see another story? That's success too. That's the beginning of word-of-mouth.

---

## The half-that-buys metric

**Who is most likely to convert:**
- Developers who care about privacy and native apps
- Designers who respond to the visual and narrative quality
- Anyone who had a real answer to "where did you come from" and felt something when they saw it reflected back

**Who is least likely to convert (and what to do about them):**
- People who "don't play games" — the demo reframes it. It's not a game, it's a story with choices.
- People who question the $19.99 — compare it to a book. A book about these topics would cost $25 and tell you someone else's conclusions. TERRA asks for yours.
- People who need to "think about it" — that's fine. Give them the press kit. Let them sleep on it.

---

## What not to do

- Don't show them the code (unless they specifically ask and are developers)
- Don't give them a feature list before they've played the demo
- Don't compare TERRA to other games — it's not competing with those
- Don't use the word "educational" — that signals homework, not experience
- Don't follow up more than once after Day 2. If they're interested, they'll come back.

---

## The underlying truth

The new team will buy it if they feel what the game is about — not if they understand what it is.

TERRA is about the experience of holding something difficult and still choosing to act. The 3-day process IS the game, in miniature:

- Day 1: You're given a question you didn't ask for
- Day 2: You encounter something that makes you feel something
- Day 3: Your answer comes back to you

If the 3 days are designed right, they won't be buying an app. They'll be completing a circuit.
