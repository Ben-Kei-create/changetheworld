Climate Crisis West Africa

The Sun Never Forgets

Amara Diallo, 28 · Solar Engineer · Senegal

She has 11 solar panels, 3 batteries, and a proposal to power 40 villages. The regional authority wants a bribe to approve it. She has 48 hours.

Africa contributes <4% of global CO₂ — yet bears the greatest climate burden.
Ocean Pollution East Asia

What the Water Remembers

Mei Lin, 19 · Researcher · Shanghai

She has mapped 847 microplastic sample points along the Yangtze estuary. Her professor wants her to soften the conclusions. The data is already out.

170 million metric tons of plastic are in our oceans. Growing by 11M tons per year.
Deforestation South America

The Forest That Breathes

Carlos Vega, 45 · Land Defender · Peru

His community has protected the same 2,000 hectares for generations. An illegal logging road arrived three months ago. He has 72 hours before the next crew.

Indigenous peoples protect 80% of remaining biodiversity while managing 22% of land.
Inequality Middle East

A Tent Is Not a Ceiling

Fatima Al-Hassan, 34 · Teacher · Jordan

She teaches 60 children in a tent school at a refugee camp on the Syrian border. A student named Nour has underlined Emmy Noether: "She lectured anyway."

250 million school-age children are not in school globally.
Poverty West Africa

Roots in Concrete

James Okafor, 55 · Urban Farmer · Lagos

He converted an abandoned lot into a food forest feeding 500 families. A developer bought the adjacent land yesterday. The relocation offer is 14 km away — inaccessible by transit.

Urban food forests produce up to 6× more food per m² than industrial agriculture.
Isolation East Asia

The Space Between Words

Hana Nakamura, 72 · Retired · Tokyo

She hasn't had a conversation longer than ninety seconds in three years. She decided to build a community room in her building's unused storage space. Management has said no twice. She has written four letters. She has more patience than the building has forms.

1 in 4 adults globally feel chronically lonely. Japan appointed a Minister of Loneliness in 2021.
Future & Fear Europe

Still Here

Astrid Lindqvist, 16 · Student · Malmö, Sweden

It is 2:47 AM and Astrid has not slept well in two years. She knows what tipping points are. She knows what irreversible means. She learned these things because she asked. She is past panic. She is in the still, cold place after panic. Her phone lights up. Her friend Sara: "Still awake?"

59% of young people globally are extremely worried about climate change. The Baltic Sea has warmed faster than almost any body of water on Earth.
WHAT KIND OF GAME IS THIS

No combat.
No timers.
No lives.

TERRA is a narrative experience with branching choices. You read. You decide. You live with the outcome.

Every decision has weight — not because the game punishes you, but because the situations are real and the people who face them are real. We wrote each story with that in mind.

It takes 60–90 minutes per story. There are six stories. Most people don't play them in one sitting.

"I read Hana's story and sat quietly for ten minutes afterward. Then I called my mother."
— Early playtester

WHY WE BUILT THIS

We kept reading the news and feeling helpless.
Then we realized: that's not the right feeling to leave people with.

TERRA started from a question: what if a game could make you understand a problem so deeply that you felt connected to it — not overwhelmed by it?

We researched each character for months. We read studies, found primary sources, and looked for the specific moments where individual choice meets systemic pressure. Those moments became the game.

We are a small team. This is our first game. We made it because we needed it to exist.
